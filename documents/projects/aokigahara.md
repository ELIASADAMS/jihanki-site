## PROJECT STATEMENT

AOKIGAHARA is a procedural ambient sound system imagining the forest around Mount Fuji as a living memory.

The work is not designed as a fixed sequence of songs. It is built as a network of interconnected generative processes that model environmental activity, human traces, memory, ritual and mechanical repetition.

Musical fragments can be stored, aged, mutated and recalled. Rhythms evolve through probabilistic interactions and micro-temporal variation. Human traces can become indistinguishable from environmental sounds, while a fictional Kagura Machine continues to reconstruct a ritual after its original meaning has disappeared.

The work is influenced by Japanese minimalism, experimental electronic music, procedural composition, and the atmosphere of the forests and lakes around Mount Fuji.

> It begins as a landscape.
> It becomes a memory.
> It becomes a ritual.
> It becomes a machine.
> Eventually, it forgets itself.

## SCIENTIFIC FRAMING

The word **scientific** is used as a method of description, not as a claim that the patches are literal ecological or neurological simulations.

The system uses scientific concepts as compositional models:

- **State** — the current condition of a process.
- **Event** — a discrete sonic action generated when conditions allow it.
- **Probability** — a control mechanism determining whether an event occurs.
- **Persistence** — retention of information across successive events.
- **Decay** — gradual reduction of energy, reliability or density.
- **Mutation** — controlled change applied to remembered material.
- **Feedback** — a process responding to the activity of another process.
- **Emergence** — audible structure produced by interactions rather than by a fixed score.

The system therefore resembles a distributed dynamical model: each process has local rules, while the resulting composition is determined by interaction over time.

## COMPOSITIONAL PRINCIPLES

1. Events arise from current conditions rather than from a predetermined timeline.
2. Random choices remain bounded by musical, temporal and timbral rules.
3. Previous events influence later events.
4. Multi-scale time allows micro-rhythms, gestures, memories and environmental changes to operate at different rates.
5. Silence and low event density are active structural conditions.
6. No single loop completely determines the form.
7. The ending is generated by loss of activity rather than imposed as an external stop.

## SHARED STATE MODEL

**activity** — 0.0–1.0 — current event capacity

**memory_reliability** — 0.0–1.0 — probability of successful recall

**instability** — 0.0–1.0 — timing and parameter drift

**spectral_brightness** — 0.0–1.0 — high-frequency presence

**human_presence** — 0.0–1.0 — density of human traces

**ritual_intensity** — 0.0–1.0 — degree of patterned repetition

**corruption** — 0.0–1.0 — deviation from remembered material

**residue** — 0.0–1.0 — surviving environmental activity

**dissolution** — 0.0–1.0 — degree of systemic disappearance

These values are compositional controls, not real measurements.

## EVENT MODEL

↓
WAIT ACCORDING TO LOCAL TIME SCALE
↓
POSSIBLY GENERATE AN EVENT
↓
STORE, MODIFY OR TRANSMIT INFORMATION
↓

For example, an imagined dissolution relationship can be expressed as:

activity = 1 - dissolution

probability = base_probability × activity

interval = base_interval × (1 + dissolution × instability_factor)

The equations are not intended as numerical realism. They make the intended musical behaviour explicit and reproducible.

## WHY IT IS NOT SIMPLY RANDOM

Unconstrained randomness produces unrelated events.

AOKIGAHARA uses bounded choices, persistent state, recurrence, decay, mutation and cross-process response. The system therefore has history: the present is partly determined by what has already occurred.

A random generator forgets immediately.

AOKIGAHARA remembers imperfectly.

## TEMPORAL ECOLOGY

The system occupies several temporal scales:

- milliseconds to fractions of a second — micro-timing and small deviations;
- seconds — gestures, pulses and responses;
- tens of seconds — phrases, echoes and ritual cycles;
- minutes — memory survival, environmental change and dissolution.

This produces a temporal ecology in which geological, biological, human and ritual times can coexist without being reduced to a common beat.

## THE FIVE ENVIRONMENTS

### 01 — FUJISAN

**Geological time**

Mass, pressure, long cycles and extremely slow transformation.

### 02 — AOKIGAHARA

**Biological time**

Growth, density, decay, hidden activity and environmental memory.

### 03 — SAIKO

**Reflective time**

Water, echoes, distance, reflection and memory returning from elsewhere.

### 04 — VILLAGE

**Social time**

Human traces, infrastructure, mechanical repetition and abandoned routines.

### 05 — SHRINE

**Ritual time**

Bells, recurrence, ceremony and the fictional Kagura Machine.

## HUMAN TRACE

Human presence is treated as another process rather than a separate soundtrack.

Possible traces include footsteps, distant voices, vehicles, bicycles, machinery, vending machines, bells, infrastructure, electrical hum and recorded field sound.

At sufficient procedural distance, the distinction between environmental sound and human sound should become uncertain.

A mechanical repetition may begin to sound organic.

An organic texture may begin to sound mechanical.

## RITUAL AND THE KAGURA MACHINE

Eventually its original meaning can disappear while the pattern survives.

## DISSOLUTION

The system does not end simply because an external command says STOP.

Instead, the conditions that allow new events to occur gradually disappear.

ACTIVE
↓
UNSTABLE
↓
FRAGMENTING
↓
FORGETTING
↓
RESIDUE
↓
SILENCE

During dissolution:

- activity becomes sparse;
- memory recall becomes less reliable;
- timing becomes increasingly unstable;
- high frequencies disappear first;
- drones and reverberation remain longer;
- micro-events mutate or fail to occur;
- existing loops terminate after their remaining sound has decayed.

The forest does not become quiet because something externally silences it. It becomes unable to produce new events.
