# 2026-07-26 — TOYS / RITUALS / SHRINES

## 12:00 — Extending Car Rental

The car rental was extended for another week.

## 12:30 — Kawaguchiko Retro Toys Museum

The museum contained many simple electronic toys that looked similar to Soviet electronic toys. There was also a simple video game on a Family Computer-like system connected to an old TV.

In the souvenir shop Julia found a lace for symbolic charms with kanji, UFO imagery and other motifs. Eli found an opossum.

We recorded the sound of the Kawaguchiko tower clock for the first time.

There were many monster toy figures aligned in long rows. In this arrangement they did not look scary at all, unlike how frightening they might appear in chaos.

The meaning was not yet clear, but it suggested something like **ordinary monsters**.

The eerie feeling of being inside a game continued.

## 14:30 — First Shrine Visit

**Fujisan Sengen Shrine.**

We began learning shrine rituals and bought two books for collecting shrine calligraphic pages.

They looked completely *onaji*, so we needed to sign them with our names. Julia spent a long time deciding which name to use and finally wrote the obtained name **タキスマナ (Takismana)**.

This became a turning point: the name became attached to something important within the expedition's ritual life.

## 22:00 — Night Drive / Arakura Shrine

We drove to a local village and then Arakura Shrine.

The village itself was not impressive; we had already encountered it during the Lake Saiko night walk. What remained in the photographs were three jihankis, combining ordinary and surreal feelings: their lights shining in a completely empty parking area.

Arakura Shrine was 20–30 minutes away by car, high in the mountains and completely empty. The view included Fuji, an almost full Moon, and the lights of local towns and villages.

We recorded train sounds from the town that could be heard from the mountain.

We learned that the centre of a shrine should not be photographed. Later Koba-san told us that shrines should not be visited after sunset because the protective spirits are sleeping.

### Recurring motifs reinforced

- Jihanki lights.
- Night roads.
- Shrines and ritual.
- Sound travelling across distance.
- The mountain as an active presence.
- Rules and thresholds around sacred spaces.
