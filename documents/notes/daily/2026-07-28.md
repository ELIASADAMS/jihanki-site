# 2026-07-28 — TOKYO / ACCEPTED & REJECTED PLACES

## Second Trip to Tokyo

The second trip was more organized than the first. We already knew the rules of the game: roads, parking, transportation, where to eat and what we wanted to do.

We arrived at Harajuku Station and first went to a large shrine and park. It was not bad, but it was spoiled by tourists, and the tremulous feeling we had experienced in local shrines was missing.

Suddenly our mood almost synchronously dropped. Something felt wrong, so we left.

Maybe Tokyo was simply not accepting us.

## Harajuku / Searching for Independent Music

We continued through the lively streets of Harajuku and bought some stylish clothes.

We were still looking for independent music shops, ordinary music shops, and other possible connections through which we could share our CDs, cassettes, drawings and comicbooks with Japanese people.

This time we were more lucky.

A girl in a music shop recognized **Gakupo** in Taki's earrings. That became the clue that allowed the conversation to continue.

We shared Eli's Kasane Teto drawings first — it always works — then our music, and then asked for further contacts with galleries, concerts and other places.

The gallery sidequest was not very successful, but it was still much better than the first attempt in Akihabara.

In the end we managed to leave jazz CDs in a good jazz music shop with a very friendly and jazz-loving guy. Hopefully they are now in good hands.

## The Way Back

The way back from Tokyo was long and difficult as always.

This time we stopped several times near jihankis and rivers to regain concentration.

The feeling that a jihanki was not simply a vending machine became stronger and stronger, although the idea had still not been clearly formulated.

That happened the next day.

### Observations

- Places can accept or reject you through their atmosphere. Sudden mood changes became part of how we perceived locations.
- **Gakupo** became another digital spirit and guide, opening doors that were otherwise difficult to enter.
- **Jihanki** increasingly felt like a place with a friendly presence rather than ordinary infrastructure.
