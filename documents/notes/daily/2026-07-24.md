# 2026-07-24 — TOKYO / THE INVISIBLE CITY

## 12:13 — Kawaguchiko Station

We travelled to Kawaguchiko Station in the Fuji Five Lakes area. Again, the most catchy signs around the bus stop were jihankis. In daylight they looked kawaii, friendly and ordinary.

The personal Thursday ritual of drinking coffee with cinnamon continued, bringing a familiar habit into an unfamiliar environment.

## 13:10 — Rental Car

Julia rented a compact Honda for four days. The car transformed the scale of the expedition. Until then we had mainly explored by bicycle around Lake Saiko; now the Mount Fuji region, and eventually Tokyo, became accessible.

The car was simple and practical. Its fuel consumption was unexpectedly low, and the experience made Eli want to learn to drive.

Every road around Mount Fuji became a possible route.

## Fuji-area shrine pilgrimage

The day gradually turned into an impromptu pilgrimage to several local Shinto shrines. After dark, reaching the summit of one surrounding mountain produced an intense feeling that was difficult to explain. Nothing supernatural happened, but the atmosphere felt as if the landscape had shifted into another dimension.

In retrospect, this was one of the first moments when the journey stopped feeling like tourism and started feeling like an expedition.

## 13:55 — First Exploration of Aokigahara

We visited Aokigahara Forest and its lava caves for the first time and explored two caves. They were impressive but crowded, making isolation difficult.

Before going deeper into the forest, an audio recorder was placed behind some trees. We walked further for about an hour. When we returned, the recorder had captured around an hour of uninterrupted forest ambience.

### Themes

- Listening instead of observing.
- The forest as an autonomous actor.
- Delegating perception to machines.

## 17:05 — Otsuka and the Beginning of the Concept

We encountered vending machines carrying the name Otsuka. Initially we assumed Otsuka referred only to the company. Exploring further, we noticed nearby names: Otsuki, Mount Eboshidake, Yamanakaka, and Lake Motosuko.

This coincidence sparked one of the project's first major conceptual ideas:

# The Six Great Yōkai of Mount Fuji

Instead of documenting isolated legends, we began imagining the Fuji region as a constellation of contemporary yōkai, each connected to a landscape, infrastructure or recurring encounter.

Lake Motosuko immediately stood out as a place worth revisiting.

**This was the first moment when the project transformed from documentation into mythology.**

## 18:09 — Lake Motosuko

First visit to Lake Motosuko. After buying ice cream with zundamon-like filling, we spent the evening quietly on the lakeshore.

Unlike the intensity of Aokigahara, the lake felt calm.

### Observations

- Evening light over the water.
- Silence after a long day of movement.
- Strong fatigue after the first days in Japan.
- The lake remained in memory long after leaving.

## 19:33 — First Close Encounter with Shiki

On the road we had our first close meeting with Shiki, when we managed to look closely and make videos.

The encounter remained undefined, but significant enough to record. Rather than explaining it immediately, its meaning remained open.

## 09:38 — Departure for Tokyo

The first road trip to Tokyo began. The transition from the forests around Mount Fuji to the metropolis became another threshold within the expedition.

## 12:06 — Entering Tokyo

Mountains, forests and lakes gradually gave way to dense urban infrastructure.

## 12:20 — Arrival

We parked the car using a difficult parking system; a man on a motorbike helped us. We stopped at McDonald's and entered Tokyo's dense metro system.

Japanese McDonald's discoveries included ShakaChiki chicken and edamame salad.

## 14:45 — Akihabara

Akihabara felt overwhelmingly geared toward tourists and surprisingly disconnected from what we had been searching for. We acquired:

- High-quality headphones.
- A Hatsune Miku figure.
- A trackball.

The Miku figure echoed the image that welcomed us at Haneda: a digital presence had crossed from the screen into the journey.

## 18:05 — Searching for the Invisible Akihabara

We searched for independent music releases, obscure electronics, synthesizers and dōjin music. Instead we repeatedly encountered anime merchandise, figurines, games and dōjinshi.

The word 同人 (dōjin) was repeatedly understood as 同人誌 (dōjinshi), so we began asking for **同人の音楽** or **自分出版**.

The district had become overwhelmingly mainstream. Despite being associated internationally with otaku culture and electronics, we did not find the musical underground we had imagined.

### Observations

- Expectation collided with reality.
- The underground remained hidden.
- Language became an obstacle.
- Absence proved more meaningful than discovery.
- The invisible underground.
- The disappearance of cultural spaces.

## 18:30 — Dreamtonics Headquarters

We visited Dreamtonics headquarters. Eli gave a speech and obtained a pair of Miku glasses, which unexpectedly became part of his visual identity.

A digital character had crossed from the screen into the physical journey itself.

## 22:40–03:00 — Return to Saikoneon

The return from Tokyo began smoothly. We found a free road to drive without payment. Miku's figure was on the car's front panel.

Julia noticed that leaving Tokyo's districts and suburbs felt like leaving endless loops: every quarter looked similar to another and seemed never to end. From this point, the song **“Looping rooms”** became a symbol of leaving Tokyo.

After leaving the Tokyo districts, the road looked strangely game-like. Eli remembered having played a computer game with a similar road. The feeling of being inside a game became a recurring sensation during the trip.

Later Eli developed a strong headache and fever and, while fever-talking, mentioned yōkai and ways of communicating with them. Julia reached Saikoneon extremely tired and running on adrenaline.

---

**Project shift:** the journey began connecting physical landscape, digital spirits, infrastructure and the feeling of navigating a game.
