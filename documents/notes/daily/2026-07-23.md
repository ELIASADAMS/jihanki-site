# 2026-07-23 — THE LOCAL MAP

## 10:18 — Message from Koba-san

After discussing our plans, Koba-san sent a detailed guide to the Saiko area and surrounding places.

It was more than a tourist recommendation. It became the first real map of the landscape from someone who lived there. Until then, we had mostly stumbled upon vending machines in the dark, forgotten infrastructure, forest paths and strange encounters. Now we received a local person's interpretation of the mountain.

## Recommended Places Around Saiko

### Iyashi no Sato

A reconstructed traditional village on the opposite side of Lake Saiko. Koba-san emphasised traditional crafts, handmade objects and local culture.

**Possible connection:**

- Preserved memory.
- Artificial reconstruction of the past.
- Whether tradition remains alive when recreated.

### Ryugu Cave

A small and less famous cave near Saiko, recommended for its atmosphere and the small shrine dedicated to spring water flowing from the cave.

This became important because it represented a relationship with nature in which water sources, mountains, forests and natural formations can become objects of respect and worship.

**Possible themes:**

- Natural places as living entities.
- Water spirits.
- Caves as entrances between worlds.

### Kitaguchi Hongu Fuji Sengen Shrine

A major sacred place connected with Mt. Fuji worship. Koba-san offered to take us there together with the Fujisan Museum.

**Possible themes:**

- Fuji as a sacred mountain.
- Pilgrimage.
- Human relationships with landscape.

### Fujisan Museum

A museum dedicated to the history of Mt. Fuji worship from the Yamanashi side. Koba-san noted that it did not focus heavily on yōkai, but thought we might appreciate its connection with animism and traditional views of nature.

This marked an important distinction: the project was not only about monsters or ghosts. It was gradually becoming about the Japanese idea that landscapes themselves can possess presence.

## Koba-san's Cultural Recommendations

Koba-san shared that as a child he loved *GeGeGe no Kitarō* and other anime about yōkai. As an adult his interests shifted toward modern Japanese ghost stories and horror films.

Recommendations included:

- *Ju-On: Origins*
- Modern Japanese ghost stories
- *Pom Poko*
- *Spirited Away*
- *Hakaba Kitarō*
- Films by Akio Jissoji, especially his feature films rather than his *Ultraman* work.

The physical guide to Saiko therefore became a cultural map of Japanese supernatural imagination as well.
