# 2026-07-29 — RYUGU CAVE / KOYODAI

## Afternoon Hike

**Ryugu Cave – Mount Koyodai Loop, Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park**

The hike followed the route previously recommended by Koba-san.

The route incorporated features that had already become integral to the expedition:

- Underground spaces.
- Mountain paths.
- Forests.
- Water sources.
- Hidden locations away from major tourist routes.

Unlike the crowded caves visited earlier in Aokigahara, this route had a quieter and more intimate atmosphere. The first part was physically demanding and marked a transition from ordinary walking to venturing deeper into the mountains.

Ryugu Cave reinforced a recurring pattern: a place initially appears as a simple location on a map, then transforms into something else when entered.

The cave was not only a geological formation. It was also associated with water, worship and the relationship between humans and the natural world. This connected directly with Koba-san's explanation that a small shrine was dedicated to the spring inside the cave.

## Evolution of the Yōkai Concept

The concept of yōkai evolved during the expedition. They were not necessarily creatures lurking somewhere.

They could also represent:

- The atmosphere of a place.
- Memories associated with a place.
- The notion that landscapes have character.

Ryugu Cave reinforced a core belief: the most interesting places were often not famous tourist attractions, but locations where nature, history and human imagination overlapped.

**The yōkai were not found. They were gradually created.**
