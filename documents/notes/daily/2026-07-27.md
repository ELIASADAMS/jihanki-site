# 2026-07-27 — MANYŌ / MOUNTAIN ASCENT

## 08:00 — Morning Dance

Another regular Taki habit began: a morning dance in Saikoneon on the wooden stage in the mountain forest.

Taki's period of sadness disappeared for a couple of weeks.

## 13:44 — Going Up to the Mountains

We started ascending directly from Saikoneon. It was a challenging route.

Eli found a metal hook, which became our lucky thing during the day. With the hook he helped Taki climb the sliding ground of the mountain.

Eventually we found a trail. It led through the mountain ridge to two important checkpoints:

- a stone with a Manyōshū tanka;
- a viewpoint with a wooden construction where clouds were passing through the landscape.

At the viewpoint we stopped to watch the running clouds, which seemed close enough to reach by hand, and recorded many videos. Some of these videos were later used by Taki in the project.

## The Manyōshū Stone

The story connected with the stone was not yet finished within the project. The source preserves the archaic text because it remained important to the expedition.

The poem is traditionally associated with Mount Fuji and describes the mountain as something beyond ordinary language: a sacred and mysterious presence, impossible to name adequately.

うち寄する 駿河の国と  
こちごちの 国のみ中ゆ  
出で立てる 富士の高嶺は

天雲も い行きはばかり  
飛ぶ鳥も 飛びも上らず  
燃ゆる火を 雪もち消ち  
降る雪を 火もち消ちつつ

言ひも得ず 名付けも知らず  
くすしくも います神かも  
石花の海と 名付けてあるも  
その山の つつめる海ぞ

富士川と 人の渡るも  
その山の 水のたぎちぞ  
日の本の 大和の国の  
鎮めとも います神かも

宝とも なれる山かも  
駿河なる 富士の高嶺は  
見れど飽かぬかも

## The Story of the Stone

According to the story recorded in the notes, the stone was installed in the 1960s by a professor of literature from outside Japan who walked this route with his Japanese friend. They stopped at the viewpoint with a beautiful view of Fuji, and the Japanese friend quoted the Manyōshū poem.

Several years later the friend died, and the professor installed the stone at exactly that place.
