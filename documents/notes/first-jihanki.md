# FIRST JIHANKI

**Date:** 2026-07-22  
**Time:** 02:00  
**Location:** SAIKONEON / Lake Saiko, Yamanashi, Japan  
**Participants:** Ilya Minin (Eli) & Julia Baranyuk  
**Type:** Field note  
**Tags:** jihanki, night, infrastructure, threshold, saikoneon, japan-expedition

## Origin

The JIHANKI project began at **SAIKONEON**, an artist-in-residence on the shore of Lake Saiko, during the July 2026 Japan stay shared by Ilya Minin (Eli) and Julia Baranyuk.

SAIKONEON is a place built around research, creation, exchange, and encounters between artists. The landscape around the residence — the lake, forest, mountain roads, small settlements, and isolated infrastructure — became part of the material of the trip rather than simply its backdrop.

JIHANKI emerged from that environment as a way of preserving the strange encounters that happened there. The project takes one ordinary object encountered during the stay, the Japanese vending machine, and turns it into a fictional archive interface.

The name **JIHANKI (自販機)** refers to the Japanese word for vending machine. The physical machines encountered around Saiko became the conceptual seed of the project.

## The first night-time walk

The first encounter was with a jihanki, a Japanese vending machine.

At approximately 02:00, during a night walk around SAIKONEON and Lake Saiko, the machine appeared as an isolated illuminated object in the darkness.

What initially looked like ordinary infrastructure suddenly felt detached from its practical purpose. It had a screen, lights, sounds, buttons, money, and a mechanism for returning something to a person — but at night it could also be experienced as a small autonomous system occupying an otherwise empty landscape.

That encounter became the starting point for the JIHANKI archive.

## Observation

At night, the vending machine felt less like a shop than an illuminated object existing alone in darkness.

Its isolation was important. The machine was not interesting because it was unusual technology; it was interesting because an everyday technological object became uncanny through context, distance, darkness, and repetition.

During the following walks around Saiko, more vending machines appeared. They were not identical: different models, colours, sounds, products, interfaces, and accepted forms of payment. The repeated object therefore became a kind of local vocabulary.

## From encounter to archive

The vending machine became more than a subject of observation. It became a model for how the whole trip could be remembered.

A vending machine accepts a small input and returns a selected object. JIHANKI reinterprets that mechanism as an archive:

```text
small action
    ↓
machine responds
    ↓
a memory appears
    ↓
the visitor chooses another memory
```

Instead of purchasing a drink or snack, the visitor enters the machine and receives fragments of the journey: photographs, sounds, music, field notes, scripts, videos, experiments, and observations.

The machine is therefore both **interface and metaphor**.

## JIHANKI project connection

The vending machine becomes an archive interface: an object that accepts a small payment and returns a fragment of memory.

The final website deliberately preserves the feeling of a machine rather than presenting the material as a conventional documentation page. The archive is the hidden body of the project; the vending machine is the visible surface through which it is accessed.
