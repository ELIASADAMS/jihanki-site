AOKIGAHARA

PROCEDURAL AMBIENT PENTALOGY

A procedural ambient sound system imagining the forest around Mount Fuji as a living memory. It is not a fixed sequence of songs: it is a network of generative processes that accumulate, remember, mutate, ritualise and eventually forget.

It begins as a landscape.It becomes a memory.It becomes a ritual.It becomes a machine.Eventually, it forgets itself.

PROJECT STATEMENT

The project began with a simple act of listening. During the first exploration of Aokigahara, a recorder was left in the forest while the forest was explored without it. The returned recording suggested a different relationship between observer and environment: the machine had listened without human intention.

AOKIGAHARA extends that idea into procedural composition. Instead of reproducing the forest literally, the system asks what might happen if a musical environment could remember, forget, mutate its own history, and continue a ritual after its meaning had disappeared.

The scientific vocabulary used here describes compositional behaviour. It is not a claim that Sonic Pi is a literal ecological, geological or neurological simulation.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

The project behaves as a distributed system rather than a linear arrangement. Each process follows local rules while responding to shared conditions. The global composition emerges from their interaction.

WHY IT IS NOT SIMPLY RANDOM

Unconstrained randomness produces unrelated events. AOKIGAHARA uses bounded choices, persistent state, recurrence, decay, mutation and cross-process response. The present behaviour is partly determined by what has already occurred.

DISSOLUTION

The system does not end because an external command silences it. The conditions required to generate new events gradually disappear.

SILENCE

During dissolution, activity becomes sparse, memory recall becomes unreliable, timing becomes increasingly unstable, high frequencies disappear first, while drones and reverberation survive longer. Existing loops terminate only after their remaining sound has decayed.

COMPOSITIONAL PRINCIPLES

#PRINCIPLE
01Events arise from current conditions rather than a predetermined timeline.
02Random choices remain bounded by musical, temporal and timbral rules.
03Previous events influence later events.
04Different temporal scales coexist.
05Silence and low event density are active structural conditions.
06No single loop completely determines the form.
07Memory may return in altered form.
08The ending is produced by loss of activity rather than an imposed stop.
